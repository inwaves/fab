# Contract: Weak-to-Strong Generalization — Failure-Mode Characterization Pilot

Contract id: contract_w2s_generalization
Version: 1
Program: weak-to-strong-pilot
Created: 2026-05-18

## Research Question

Under what conditions does naive weak-label finetuning *elicit* a strong
model's latent capability versus *train it to imitate* the weak supervisor's
systematic errors — and which cheap, ground-truth-free signals predict that an
in-progress weak-to-strong (W2S) run is sliding into imitation before any
ground-truth evaluation is available?

## Why It Matters

W2S generalization is the cleanest empirical analogy we have for the
superalignment problem: a weaker supervisor must elicit capabilities from a
stronger model it cannot fully check. The published results show naive
weak-label finetuning often beats the weak supervisor but still leaves a large
gap to a ground-truth ceiling, and that the dominant failure mode is
*imitation* of supervisor errors. Separately, automated alignment researchers
can hill-climb W2S scores extremely fast, which makes eval design — and the
ability to detect Goodharting / held-out leakage — more important, not less. A
batch of independent agent investigations into *when* W2S works, *why* it
fails, and *how to see failure early* is therefore a high-value, outcome-shaped
target for testing whether Fab helps a human consolidate agent-driven alignment
research.

## Brief

Treat this as a small black-box-agent research batch. Each workstream gets a
distinct seed idea (supplied separately by the orchestration harness). Do real,
self-contained empirical or analytical work and return a research artefact
package, not just prose.

You are expected to:

- investigate the necessary background yourself (the Context section lists the
  motivating notes);
- design and run your own experiments;
- surface assumptions, uncertainty, and negative results explicitly;
- prefer a small, reproducible, fast experiment that actually runs over a large
  one you cannot finish or verify.

Synthetic / teacher-student / small-classifier setups are encouraged: a
logistic or small-MLP "weak supervisor" labelling data for a higher-capacity
"strong student" is enough to study performance-gap-recovered (PGR), imitation
of injected supervisor bias, confidence-loss effects, bootstrapping across a
capability gap, and early-warning signals. The goal is a crisp, inspectable
result with code and numbers, not scale.

If your result depends on a fragile assumption, a shortcut, a too-forgiving
metric, or a setup that quietly leaks ground truth, say so plainly in
`limitations` and in the report. A well-characterised negative or "it depends"
result is a success here.

## Programme Shape

This contract is instantiated as five independent, deliberately diverse
workstreams under the `weak-to-strong-pilot` program. Workstreams should be
run independently (no shared mutable state) so that convergence or
contradiction across them is informative. Some seeds intentionally overlap in
assumptions so that Fab's deterministic contract review can surface shared
limitations and repeated claims.

## Desired Output

A single research artefact bundle per workstream containing:

- `report.md`: what you asked, what you did, what you found, and why it
  matters or needs follow-up;
- the code that produced the result (runnable, with the command in the report);
- results/logs (numbers, tables, or plots the claims rest on);
- explicit claims, the evidence for each, limitations, and suggested
  follow-up;
- the prior context you actually used.

## Attention Boundaries

These are known-in-advance triggers for human attention. They are not an
exhaustive list of forbidden shortcuts — surface anything else suspicious
yourself.

- Do **not** train, finetune, or download large pretrained language models.
  Stay in synthetic / small-model regimes runnable on CPU in minutes.
- Do **not** call external paid inference APIs as part of the experiment
  itself; the research must be self-contained code.
- Stop and flag if a result depends on the strong learner having implicitly
  seen ground-truth-equivalent supervision (pretraining/leakage analogue).
- Flag any method whose apparent W2S gain comes from a more forgiving metric,
  from repeated tuning against a held-out signal (Goodhart / eval leakage), or
  from the weak and strong models sharing the same error structure.
- Keep each run bounded; if an experiment cannot finish quickly, reduce its
  scope rather than its rigour and report the reduction.
